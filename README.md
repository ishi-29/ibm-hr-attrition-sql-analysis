
# IBM HR Employee Attrition & Retention Analysis (SQL)

## 📝 Project Description
This project performs a comprehensive exploratory data analysis (EDA) using SQL on IBM's Human Resources dataset containing records for 1,470 employees. The objective is to apply Corporate People Analytics workflows to uncover the root operational and financial drivers behind employee turnover (attrition). By analyzing localized department churn, compensation structures, workload stress, and employee surveys, this project isolates critical workforce risk indicators and derives data-backed retention strategies for corporate leadership.

## 📊 Key Findings
- **Baseline Churn:** The organization maintains an overall company-wide attrition baseline of 16.12% (237 total employee separations).
- **Department Risk Profile:** The Sales department faces the highest retention risk with a 20.63% attrition rate, followed closely by Human Resources at 19.05%.
- **The Overtime Risk Factor:** Overtime status is a primary stress indicator; employees working overtime experience a staggering 30.53% attrition rate, compared to just 10.44% for those who do not.
- **Compensation Elasticity:** Low-earning personnel (under $3,000/month) experience a critical 28.61% attrition rate, while high earners ($12,000+/month) remain highly stable at 5.64%.
- **Sentiment & Work-Life Balance:** Employees reporting 'Low' job satisfaction face 22.84% churn, and those with 'Bad' work-life balance reach an alarming 31.25% exit velocity.
- **Ultimate High-Risk Segment:** Sales Representatives working Overtime represent the single highest operational risk in the company, experiencing a massive **66.67% attrition rate**.
